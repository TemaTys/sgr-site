Why the data can be trusted

Three pillars underpin the verifiability of the SGR project.

106M+ public records

7 official open databases, 5 U.S. metropolitan areas (Chicago, Los Angeles, New York, Philadelphia, San Francisco), 2001–2025.

Reproducible research

Open Python pipeline, pinned library versions (requirements.txt), Zenodo archive with a permanent DOI.

Independent verification welcome

Code, data, and detector thresholds are published in full; replication on other cities and countries is encouraged via GitHub Issues.

Research focus

Research focus

Research software

Data engineering for messy open municipal datasets: unification, deduplication, time-series detrending.

Time-series & statistics

Random-effects meta-analysis with HKSJ correction, Hedges' g, BH-FDR, robustness batteries (permutation, bootstrap, placebo).

Chronobiology

Astronomical engine built on Skyfield and JPL DE421 ephemerides; a ddF detector for impulsive lunar tidal waves.

AI-assisted workflows

Using modern AI tooling to accelerate development, documentation, and reproducibility of research code.

Bio (EN)

Artem Tysiatskii (TemaTys) is an independent researcher and software developer based in Kaliningrad, Russia. Author of Seleno-Gravitational Rhythm (SGR), an open pipeline and cross-city meta-analysis of impulsive lunar tidal waves across 106M+ records of U.S. crime and medical statistics (2001–2025). Publishes code, data, and articles openly on GitHub and Zenodo.
